# Usage Guide

A quick run through what Smash does.

## Sessions

Everything you log lives here. Tap the plus button in the bottom right to open a full page, pick Singles, Doubles or Training, and the form opens straight away. The list behind it shows play sessions, scored matches and training together, newest first.

## Players

Add the people you play with here, with an optional level and a note. When you log a match, choose a saved player for the opponent or partner and the name and level fill in for you. Court-time sessions can use the same list.

## Play

The Court time form holds both the session and its score. Date defaults to today, Start time follows your 12- or 24-hour setting, and Duration on Court is a whole number of hours. Game Record holds the point system, format, intensity and opponent; doubles also asks for a partner. Tap Edit score for the point wheels, and add a row for every game in continuous play. Pick an opponent before saving. Court time and training are counted as sessions in your weekly chart and toward your activity goal — the chart is "in number of sessions", not minutes.

## Training

Record your drill sessions. Add as many drills as you worked on — pick each one from the inline menu or type a custom name — then set its own sets completed and difficulty. Focus areas and a note on what to repeat next time sit alongside them. There's no separate drill library to browse.

## Matches

Scored matches are entered inside the Court time form, not in a separate tab. Score continuous games or a best-of-three, with two point systems:

- **21-point rally scoring** — win by 2, capped at 30. A 30–29 finish is valid; 21–20 isn't.
- **15-point scoring** — win by 2, capped at 21. A 21–20 finish is valid.

Continuous games has no fixed game count, so add a row for every game you played.

Add intensity and your opponent's level. In doubles and mixed doubles you can add your partner too, and Smash keeps your record together. Win rate works itself out.

## Tournaments

Group matches under an event. Track your round-robin, knockout or league record, your placement, and notes for next time.

## Dashboard

See sessions this week, matches, win rate and loss rate in four cards, then goal progress below them. The activity chart counts play and training sessions — its caption reads "in number of sessions", not minutes.

**Form rating** is an Elo-style score built from your last 30 days of matches. Everyone starts at 1400. Wins lift it, losses drop it, and beating a stronger player moves it more than beating a beginner. You'll find it on the Dashboard and under **Profile → Form rating**.

**Coach's Corner** sits above the chart on the Dashboard. It picks out a handful of readable patterns from your recent matches — form, opponents, partnerships and game types — and shows the matches behind each one with a suggested thing to try. It's a prompt to think about your game, not a verdict on it.

## Personalization

- Appearance: light, dark or follow your device.
- Time format: 12-hour with an AM/PM picker, or 24-hour without one.
- A replayable app tour.

Goal cadence (off, weekly or monthly) is under Profile.

## Your data

Everything is local by default, or in your own iCloud account if you switch sync on. Export a JSON backup from Settings at any time and encrypt it with a password if you like. Importing adds records without replacing anything already there, then restores your name, skill, avatar, colour and goal from the backup. The [Privacy Policy](PRIVACY.md) has the details.

## Support

Feedback and the App Store review link are under **Profile → Support Smash**. Smash is free and open-source, with no account, no analytics and no tracking.
